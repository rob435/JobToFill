# JobToFill features

The complete feature spec: what JobToFill does on each kind of page, with the real wording of the questions it answers. The [README](../README.md) has the short version and how to install and set it up; [Privacy and security](privacy.md) says what leaves your browser. If you change how JobToFill behaves, read the section here first: every case below is something a test, or a person filling an application, relies on.

- [Cover letters and CVs](#cover-letters-and-cvs), and Quick apply

## Job applications

### Reading the form

- **Smart field detection.** Each field is scored using its `autocomplete` attribute, `<label>`, `aria-label`, placeholder, `name`/`id`, the legend of the group it sits in, ATS attributes (Workday `data-automation-id`, Lever `urls[LinkedIn]`, Greenhouse `job_application[...]`) and nearby text. Tested against Greenhouse (classic and the new job boards, with the real react-select), Ashby, Lever, Workday-style pages, Phenom (Marsh McLennan's apply steps), BambooHR (whose dropdowns are menu buttons named after the label and the choice, "Country United States", with a list that is only made when clicked), iCIMS (AMD's application, each step framed in the portal's own page), Meta Careers (whose whole site is marked hidden from screen readers while its form shows, with searchable dropdowns and a dialling code labelled only "Code"), and dozens of live graduate and internship forms listed on Trackr. A careers site's own job search is never filled with your details: Goldman Sachs' unlabelled "e.g., Asset Management, Transaction Banking…" box, WSP's "Search in city or region".
- **Built for the widgets, not the sites.** Most application forms are put together from a handful of widget libraries, so JobToFill is tested against the libraries themselves, used as their documentation shows: MUI (and MUI X's date pickers), Ant Design (and v5's Selects, whose box is a search input, as on Dayforce), Headless UI, Radix, Downshift, react-select, select2, chosen, choices.js, Tom Select, intl-tel-input, Inputmask, IMask, react-phone-number-input, jQuery UI, react-hook-form, Formik with Yup, Vue's Element Plus, react-datepicker (including month pickers that take no typing at all, as on Phenom: the year from the calendar's list, then the month), and the Quill, TipTap and Lexical editors. Each test checks what the library itself registered (its own state, its API's value, its validation errors), not just what the page shows, and that every menu is closed again. A form built on any of them, on any site, gets the same handling.
- **Everyday details, read in context.** A form's only address box (Appian's "Primary Mailing Address") gets the whole address on one line, up to the postcode when the form asks for the region and country apart. OC&C's "Known As" gets your first name (forms always get your legal name; your preferred name signs your cover letters). A bare "Title" with no job's fields around it is Mr / Ms, after "Company" the job's title. "Email address without domain" (a colleague's, in Teamtailor's "Already working at Isio? Let's recruit together"), "Referrer's email", "Emergency contact email" and "Alternate email" never get yours, even in a box made for email addresses; those, Dayforce's "Fax" and "Pager", a job's "Supervisor" and a degree's "Minor" are left for you, never sent to the AI. "If successful, where will you live during your employment?" gets your city. Yes/no questions about your studies are answered from them: "Are you currently studying or have you completed your most recent degree in the UK?", "Are you currently or have you ever attended university?", "Are you studying a MSc or other post graduate qualification?", Capgemini's "…a postgraduate qualification other than a masters degree?", Appian's "Are you currently pursuing a Major in … Computer Science or Computer Engineering", and "Please confirm that you speak English to a professional level". Microsoft Forms questions are read without the hidden note after each ("Single line text.", "Single choice."), and help text without a page's scripts or hidden error messages. Help set between a box's label and the box counts, so Teamtailor's "Start Date" with "Please indicate the earliest date you would be available to start with us." under it gets your start date, wherever it stands; a job's details beside Ashby's Application tab ("Location", "Department") are no section of its form. A field counts as required from the marks on its label too (Microsoft Forms' "Required to answer" star, Lever's "✱", Personio's "* (required)", a closing "Required"), in the page map and for the AI alike. An address "Type" (iCIMS's Mailing / Work / Home / Other) is Home, and a UK address's "Region (State, Province, County)" takes your county ("Highland"), or from a list of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland the nation your town or postcode is in ("PA34" is Scotland).
- **Questions that appear as you answer** (the ethnic background once the group is picked, "if yes" boxes, follow-ups) are filled in the same go.

### Dropdowns, boxes and editors

- **Dropdowns, radios and checkboxes.** "USA" matches "United States of America" or an option with value `US`, and "CA" matches "California". "No" matches "I am not a protected veteran", "BSc" matches "Bachelor’s", and 6 years matches the "5–10" range. Checkboxes with no names and no labels, each in a span with its text under a question (Aluna's "What's your cumulative GPA?" over "4 / 3.51 to 3.99 / …"), are one question with those options, and so are labelled ones a few wrappers below their question (Gem's "If you have held a U.S. security clearance in the past, what clearance level have you held?", where "N/A - have never held a U.S. security clearance" is ticked when you hold none). A BSc picks "College - Bachelor of Science", never the "College - Bachelor of Arts" listed first, and an A-level grade picks "A*" or "A", never the other. While your degree is under way, "Highest Education Obtained" or "What is the highest level of qualification you hold?" is "Some College" or your A-levels, never the degree you don't hold yet ("…you are currently pursuing" is the degree). "University of Glasgow" matches "Glasgow, University of", "The University of Glasgow (UofG)" or "UofG", never "Glasgow Caledonian University" or "University of Strathclyde (Glasgow)"; "UCL", "LSE", "KCL", "UC Berkeley", "MIT" and "NYU" match their full names and back. "Computing Science" picks "Computer Science" (or "STEM (Science, Technology/Computer Science, …)", or Workday's "Computer and Information Science" when that is the list's nearest), "Maths" "Mathematics" and "LLB" "Law", and a catch-all like "Science" only when nothing more telling is offered. A subject, term or language the list doesn't have picks its "Other" or "None": Economics is "Other" among Voloridge's sciences and "Other (Non-Technical)" on SpaceX's list, a June 2027 graduation "Other" among "Fall 2027 / Spring 2028…", and Aurora's "Do you speak any non-European languages…? Please select 'None' if you do not." "None". "Please indicate your first choice office location" picks your preferred city, and a list of countries (FDM's "Canada / Ireland / UK / USA") picks London's. A school entry's "Degree" is "High School Graduate" or "Secondary School" for Advanced Highers or A-levels, never another country's qualification ("Baccalaureat", "GED"). Ashby names each checkbox after its option, so a form that asks the same question twice (Redgate's equality form and its diversity survey both ask "Ethnicity", and every question offers "Prefer not to say") is still read one question at a time: each _Ethnicity_ list gets your one option, never a box from every list named alike. A Yes beside a No is one question however long the Yes runs (Redgate's "Do you require a visa…?" with "Yes (If yes, please specify the type of visa or work authorisation you would require in the box below)"), and two declarations with a box each called "I confirm" are two declarations.
- **Custom dropdowns.** Opens react-select (single and multi), Workday listboxes, Teamtailor's "Select an option" menus and async search boxes (school pickers), reads every option, picks the best match and checks the site registered it. Workday's search prompts ("School or University", "Field of Study", the phone code) get a search (Enter, as Workday wants) and a picked suggestion, clicked where a mouse would land (on the suggestion itself, not just its row), so the chip shows; "Computing Science" also searches "Computer Science". Its "Type to Add Skills" gets each of your skills, ticked one by one in the list it keeps open, and a skill Workday has already ticked from your CV is never clicked off again. A Workday dropdown ("Degree") is left to fade out after the pick, never clicked again while it closes (which would open it again over the next question). If nothing fits it leaves the box empty instead of half-typed. Long lists that only draw the rows on screen (virtual lists) are scrolled through and read in full; a widget that keeps a hidden `<select>` for the form (select2, chosen, choices.js, Tom Select) is read and checked through it; a search box that lives inside the opened menu is typed into; a list that keeps its menu open after a pick is taken for one that takes several; and a menu still fading out is waited for, never clicked open again. Dropdowns built from plain elements with no ARIA at all, just a box over the hidden input the form submits and a list of rows carrying their values (Jane Street's college, major, "How did you hear about us?", languages and dial code), are found and filled the same way, with choices kept in collapsed groups ("LinkedIn" under "Social media") read too. A hidden `<label>` that only repeats a field's name ("education_level") is not taken for its question, and "Month" and "Year" boxes side by side take the question over them ("Approximately when do you expect to begin full time employment?"). Address searches pick the suggestion in your city (Teamtailor's "12 Gower Street, London…" over the same street in Walsall), searched by your street and city without the flat number, and Lever's "Current location", whose box has no ARIA and keeps nothing typed, gets a suggestion picked too. Text typed into a pick-list that reads the same as an option is never taken for the pick itself (SuccessFactors' pick-lists keep it and still say "… is required"). iCIMS's searchable dropdowns (a hidden list behind a box that opens on a press, a "— Type to Search —" box of their own, 25 options at a time from the server, a "Region" that waits for its "Country") are opened, searched and picked as a person does, and checked against the hidden list; their search box is never taken for a question, nor their "— Make a Selection —" for an answer.
- **Custom buttons and toggles.** Yes/No toggle buttons (Ashby), `role="radio"` / `role="checkbox"` widgets (Radix, Headless UI) and switches, not just native inputs.
- **Phone numbers.** A box for the whole number ("Mobile number (inc. country code)", "Telephone number (with international dialling code)", "Mobile phone number (country code + number)", "Mobile Number (+CountryCode)") gets "+44 7700 900123", written like its example ("+447700900000" or E.164: no spaces); a separate "Country code" list gets "+44" and the box beside it the rest. "Alternative phone number", "Secondary phone" and "Landline" boxes are left empty: your number is already in the main one.
- **Lists.** Your preferred locations tick every matching office checkbox ("Which other locations would you relocate to?"), and a single "Preferred location" dropdown gets the first one it offers. A text box that names its own places ("Please rank your location preference: Austin, Chicago, Greenwich, Houston, New York") gets only the ones you prefer, or nothing. Skills checklists ("Which programming languages do you use?") and languages ("Please list any languages in which you have native or business level fluency.") work the same way.
- **Rich-text editors.** Cover letters and long answers in Quill, TipTap/ProseMirror, Lexical or a bare editable box go in as a paste, paragraphs kept, and Undo clears them.
- **Forms that change under it.** A list whose options only arrive after an earlier answer (the universities of the country you studied in, a degree level's subjects) is filled once they do. A page still drawing its form when you press _Fill_ (a spinner, a skeleton) is waited for. A number over several short boxes ("(\_\_\_) \_\_\_-\_\_\_\_", a card number in fours, a ZIP code and its +4) gets its own slice in each box, and nothing in an extension box.
- **Checks its own work.** Every answer is read back once the page has settled. A box the page emptied, put back or made anew gets its answer again (typed key by key, or pasted, when setting it from script didn't hold), and one whose rules or messages turn it down ("Please enter the date as YYYY-MM-DD", a `pattern` that wants digits only, `aria-invalid`) gets it written another way: the number without its code or spaces, the other date order or ISO, the link without `https://`, the postcode with or without its space. Whatever still isn't right is outlined in red and listed in the toast and the popup, with what the page says about it.

### Education, work history and files

- **Multiple education and job entries.** When a form repeats its _School_ or _Job title_ section, each copy gets the next entry from your profile. A section headed _Education_, _Work Experience_ or _Languages_ with fewer entries than your profile has gets its own _Add_ (_Add Another_) clicked once for each missing one first, as long as each click adds an entry (Workday's _My Experience_ starts with none); a button that opens a dialog instead is closed again and left. Each language entry gets the next language you list under Settings › _Skills & writing_ (written "English (native), Spanish (professional), basic German"): its "Language", "I am fluent in this language." ticked for a native or fluent one, and each of "Reading", "Speaking" and "Writing" at the level you gave, or the nearest below it on the form's list ("Fluent" for a native speaker when the list stops there), never above it. A level you didn't give is left for you. "From", "To" and "Location" boxes take their meaning from the section they sit in (a block that starts with its dates, as on SuccessFactors, included), and split month/year boxes are handled (Workday's "From (Actual)" and "To (Actual or Expected)" too, and their year-only "YYYY" boxes, which are hidden inputs Workday draws its "YYYY" over). A spring week or insight day that starts and ends in the same month ends the month after ("03/2026" to "04/2026"), since forms turn down a "To" that isn't after "From". A question of its own after the entries ("What year will you / did you graduate from your most recent study?") is about your degree, not the school entry just above it. A box after a list asking the same thing ("If your year of graduation is not listed, please specify.", iCIMS's "If your school is not listed, enter it here") gets your answer only when the list hasn't got it, and is never taken for a second entry. "University Course" is your subject, never the university, and "Country of School" or "Country of Employer" comes from that entry's location ("Glasgow, Scotland"; "Perth, WA" is Western Australia, "Seattle, WA" Washington). A question of its own after an unrecognised box (Chicago Trading Co's "What is your expected graduation month?" after Greenhouse's education block and an "Alternate Email") is still your degree's. "Please state your exact degree title" gets "BSc Economics", and "Please state your higher educational qualifications, including (current) grade and starting and (expected) graduating date" a line per degree ("BSc Economics, University College London, September 2024 – June 2027 (expected), predicted 2:1"). A GPA box that only takes a number (Ant Design's InputNumber on Dayforce) is left empty for a 2:1, never given "21". Secondary-school questions ("Name of secondary school", "Scottish Highers grades", "What year did you graduate from high school?") are answered from your school entry (A-levels, Highers, IB, BTEC…), never from your degree, and GCSE grades never get your A-levels. A-levels asked one box at a time (Eastdil's "A-Level Subject 2" and "A-Level Grade 2") get your second subject and grade, from "Mathematics, Further Mathematics, Economics" and "A*A*A", and a fourth is left empty for three; "Did you complete UK A-levels?" is Yes or No from your school entry, and "If you didn't complete UK A-levels, please provide your equivalent high school qualification and results" gets your other one ("Advanced Highers in Mathematics, Physics – AA") or stays empty. Boxes like these that your profile leaves empty on purpose are never reported as missing from it, nor sent to the AI. "Undergraduate University" is your university, and a required "Postgraduate University (if applicable)" gets "N/A" when you have no postgraduate degree. iCIMS's entries are answered entry by entry: "Highest Degree (Completed or Not Completed)" is that entry's degree, under way or not ("Bachelor's Degree" in the second year of a BSc, never "Some College"); "Did You Graduate?" is "In Progress" while it runs and "Received" once it has ended (never "Not Received", a degree that wasn't awarded). A date in Month / Day / Year boxes that your profile keeps to the month gets the 1st as its day ("Jun / 1 / 2028"), as iCIMS's own reading of a CV does. In a job entry, the employer's "Address", "Postal Code" and "Region" are left empty, never filled with yours; its "City" is the job's town and its "Country/Region/Location" its country (a town of the country you live in, on its own: "Aberdeen" for someone in Inverness is the United Kingdom). Entries the form has past those your profile lists (the second and third iCIMS read from a CV of one degree and a school, a language Workday read from it) are left as they are, never sent to the AI, and named in the toast: "Education entries 2 and 3 aren't in your profile: check what they hold, or remove them." With no entries in your profile at all, the AI may still fill them from your CV.
- **Referees are someone else.** A "References" block (Dayforce's First Name, Email, Mobile Phone for a referee) never gets your details, nor is it sent to the AI: its boxes are left for you, and the job entries after it still start from your first job.
- **Resume, cover letter and transcript upload.** Your files are attached to the right upload fields, even when every button just says "Attach" (Jobvite, Gem and Ashby's "autofill from resume" included). A cover-letter, transcript or code-sample upload never gets your resume (nor the CV tailored for the job), and a transcript upload for studies you don't have ("…of your graduate studies" for an undergraduate) is left empty. An upload that names your CV with other documents ("Resume/CV/Transcripts", "CV and cover letter") gets your CV first; when it takes several files (Workday's does), your cover letter goes in with it unless the form has a letter upload of its own, and your transcript when it names transcripts, all in one go. Quick apply puts them in together once the letter is written, and attaching a new letter adds just the letter, never your CV a second time. Files go in first, and if the site reads your CV and clears the form (Breezy, Lever), the answers are put back. Upload tiles that first ask where the file comes from (SuccessFactors' "Upload a Resume" → _Upload from Device / Upload from Dropbox / Sign in with Google_) get it through _Upload from Device_, without a file dialog, and so does Avature's _Upload Resume → From Device_ (Bain), whose file box only counts once its button has opened it; Dropbox, Google and other services are never chosen, and an "Additional Documents" tile never gets your resume. An upload that only says how to upload ("Select file", "Upload either DOC, DOCX, RTF, PDF, or TXT file types (1MB max)") is read by the text before it: Phenom's "An optional cover letter (or any other documents relevant to your application) can be uploaded here." and "Please upload a copy of your cover letter." get your letter, "Please upload any other relevant documents." nothing. An upload button that opens a dialog of its own instead of a file box (Eploy's "Upload CV", whose page in a frame sends the file with its "Upload File" button) is closed again and the upload left for you: a button that would send its form, that "Upload File" or a dialog's bare _Cancel_ inside the application's form, is never pressed. Dayforce's "Click Import Resume to add this information from your resume" box, which would rewrite the form from your CV, is left alone, and its "Resume Upload" box gets the CV though the two share an id: a label goes to the box it sits beside. An upload that sends its page away as soon as a file is chosen (iCIMS's "Resume" box sends the whole profile to read the CV, and the page comes back with what it read: a "+1" on a British number, the CV's town, an education entry for each line it took for one) ends that pass of the fill: once the page is back, JobToFill fills it again from your profile, over what the site read whatever Overwrite says (the site wrote it, not you), and the toast says so. The CV goes in once: an upload that lists your file by name as taken doesn't get it again, on this fill or a later one.

### Your own answers

- **Custom answers.** Rules like "_why do you want to work_" → your answer. You can match with plain phrases, `a | b` alternatives or `/regex/`.
- **Learn from this page.** After you type answers into an unusual form, one click saves them, either as profile values or as custom answers for next time.
- **Acknowledgement and terms boxes** ("I have read the privacy notice", "Acknowledge/Confirm", Oracle's "I agree with the terms and conditions") are ticked, and the toast says how many. Switch _Tick acknowledgement boxes_ off in Settings to leave them for you. DV Trading's "Terms & Conditions" list counts too. Marketing and talent-pool opt-ins ("Yes, Arma Partners can add me to the talent pool…", "Would you like to receive job alerts from us?", "Sms Consent", "Do you agree to be contacted via WhatsApp?", Redgate's "Do you agree to allow Redgate to contact you about job opportunities for up to 2 years?") are recognised and never ticked or chosen, nor passed to the AI.

### Steps, log and profiles

- **Moves through applications in steps** (Settings › _Move through multi-step applications for me_, off unless you switch it on). On an application split into steps (Workday's _My Information_, _My Experience_, _Application Questions_…), once a fill has done a step and the AI has answered what it could, JobToFill clicks the step's own _Next_ or _Save and Continue_ and fills the next step, up to 12 steps. A step the site has filled already (a returning candidate's _My Information_) is carried on too. It stops and says why, in the toast of the step's fill, at anything left for you: "Fill in “Which of our five values resonates most with you, and why?”, then press Fill to carry on.", terms to tick, an error the page shows, a CAPTCHA. While Quick apply is still writing the cover letter and CV a step uploads, that step waits for them ("JobToFill carries on to the next step once your cover letter and CV are in.") and Quick apply carries on from there. It never clicks anything that sends the application (_Submit_, _Send application_, _Finish_, _Review and submit_), and stops at the step that offers it, one without questions too (Workday's _Review_): "That’s the last step (step 4 of 4): check your application and submit it yourself." A bare _Continue_ counts only on a page that shows its steps. A step holding nothing but the employer's own questions (iCIMS's "Job Specific Questions": "Please select your level of experience or exposure to the following: FPGA") is still part of the application, by its address or the step before it, so its questions go to the AI, rather than "No form fields found"; a sign-in there (iCIMS's email-first "Login", with its list of the site's languages) is not. iCIMS's "Finish Later", which keeps the application to carry on with, sends nothing, and a progress bar that gives each step its own "Step 1 of 4" for screen readers is read at the step it marks as current ("step 3 of 4").
- **Application log.** Every job application you fill is recorded, and the log can be exported as CSV.
- **Multiple profiles.** For example "Software engineer", "Data analyst" and "Personal shopping", each with its own resume.

## Screening questions

- **Screening questions.** Answers "Are you legally authorized to work…?", "Will you require sponsorship…?", relocation, over-18, salary, notice period, non-competes, "Do you have offers from other firms or deadlines?", start date, "What is your communication preference?" (Email by default), "Are you willing to work in the office 5 days a week?", "Do you need adjustments for the recruitment process?" (and "Do you require any special requirements if you are invited to attend an interview?" in a text box), "Have you applied to us before?" (and "Have you interviewed with us before?", No when you never applied; "Have you ever applied for Graham employment before? If yes, please list dates and position." in a text box too), "Do you have any recruiting timelines we should be aware of?" and "Other Processes/Offers/Timelines" (your offers and deadlines), "Do you require a visitor visa to enter the UK for your interviews?" (No for a citizen with the right to work there; never taken for sponsorship), "Approximately when do you expect to begin full time employment?" (when your degree ends, or your start date if later), "What year did you begin your undergraduate degree?", "Your primary college/university/school email" (your university address from Settings › _Contact_, never your personal one) and "How did you hear about us?". UK forms too: right to work, your degree classification from Settings › _Education_, expected or achieved (a 2:1 picks "Upper Second Class Honours (2:1)", "2(i)" or "60-69% - Second class honours: Grade 1", never a GPA band, and a GPA box never gets "2:1" when it wants a number, nor a "GPA Scale" list anything but its UK option; Workday's "Overall Result (GPA)" gets "2:1" when the page says "…Otherwise, provide your overall result in your school/university's grading system", and so does Phenom's "What is your cumulative undergraduate (Bachelor) GPA on the scale used by your local school/university?"; "…graduate (Master) GPA… Please indicate N/A if you do not have a graduate GPA" gets "N/A" without a Master's), "Do you have a 2:1 or above?" and "Do you expect to graduate with honours?" answered from that class (a US GPA is never converted), "Will you graduate between December 2026 and July 2027?" answered from your dates, year-of-study lists, and "Are you a final year student?" or "Are you currently an undergraduate student?". It knows the trap where _"authorized to work… without sponsorship?"_ needs a **Yes**, and leaves "If you said yes above…" boxes empty when your answer was No. A question and its "If so…" in one box ("Do you have any pending offers or deadlines we should know about? If so, what companies and dates?") gets your answer; only a box of its own that starts "If so" is the follow-up. "Do you require any special requirements that we may need to be aware of?" is the adjustments question, "Were you referred by a current Cirrus Logic employee?" is answered from where you heard of the job, and "Please enter the name of the person who referred you or type, "None"" gets "None" when nobody did. Maven's "If you selected 'Other' for the above question, please specify here or write N/A if you selected another answer" gets "N/A" unless the list above it is on "Other", when it is left for you. A bar on your A-levels is answered from your school grades: Isio's "…require successful candidates to have achieved an Maths A-Level grade B or higher… Please confirm if you have obtained this." is Yes for an A* in Mathematics, and "Do you have AAB or above at A-level?" goes by your best three (a subject you didn't take is left for you). Clarity's "Current clearance level" picks "Non-clearable" for someone who holds none and isn't a US citizen (US clearances need citizenship), and Cambridge Consultants' "University event / society details" its "NA" when you heard of the job somewhere else (Maven's "Please select which event you attended" is yours: your profile doesn't say what you went to). Degrees as forms ask about them: Panmure's "Which of these categories best describes your degree?" picks "Finance or Economics" for Economics, Capgemini's "Please state your exact degree title" after its list of degrees gets "BSc Economics", Pharus's "Previous relevant role title and company (if applicable)" in one box "Summer Analyst Intern, Finch & Partners", and questions about a postgraduate degree you don't have ("What is your postgraduate degree?") are left empty rather than sent to the AI.
- **Right to work, country by country.** A question about a country you can't work in (_"Are you authorized to work in the United States?"_ for a British student) is answered **No, sponsorship needed**, while UK questions still get Yes. The country is the one the question names, or its visa words name (_"e.g., H-1B"_ is the US, _"Tier 4"_ the UK), or its options do; a question that names none (_"…in the country where this role is based?"_, or Bain's _"…in the country of any office you selected? (For example: H-1B, TN, O-1…)"_, whose visas are only examples) is about where the job is: the job already found for the tab (Quick apply, a cover letter), the location line on the application page or the posting's own words on it (Isio's "This role is based in our Birmingham office."), else the one country the form's other questions name. It goes by the countries you list, or else your nationality: EU citizens count for the whole EU/EEA, and British and Irish citizens for both countries. _"Do you hold a valid UK visa?"_ gets _"Not applicable – British/Irish citizen"_ (or No) for a British citizen, and _"Are you located in London?"_, _"Are you able to commute into our London office?"_ and _"Would you be willing to be based in our London office?"_ are answered from your address and relocation answer. "Do you possess the legal rights to work in the UK?" is the same question, and IK Partners' "What type of right to work in the UK do you hold?" picks "Citizenship/Nationality/Permanent Residency" for a British citizen (nothing for anyone else: which visa you hold isn't in your profile). A citizen's visa boxes get what they ask for: "British citizen (no visa needed)" for Arma's "…please confirm what visa type you have", "N/A" for Peel Hunt's "…If you do not require a visa, please type N/A" (someone on a visa there has details to give, so not them). "Do you hold dual nationality?" and "Do you currently hold any other (dual) nationality or citizenships?" are No with one nationality in your profile ("British, Irish" makes them Yes); citizenships held before are left for you. "Have you been employed by Capgemini Group before?" is the worked-here-before question.
- **Where you found the job.** Left blank, it's LinkedIn, however the form spells it: "Linkedin", "LinkedIn Jobs", "Social Media (LinkedIn, Instagram…)", "Job Board / LinkedIn", Workday's two-level "Social Media › LinkedIn", or just LinkedIn ticked in a "select all that apply" list. A job site the form doesn't list (Trackr, Bright Network…) picks the matching kind of option ("Online job board", "Job Board (Indeed, LinkedIn…)"), never a campus board or the employer's own website, and otherwise "Other" (an option that only ends in it, like Phenom's "Referral- Client/Vendor/Other", is a referral and never chosen: the question is left for you).
- **Dates against terms.** A May 2027 graduation picks "Spring/Summer 2027", "Spring 2027", "Q2 2027" or "Class of 2027", whichever the list offers; December picks "Fall". A year on its own counts as a June graduation. Your start date picks the first option you can make, to the day: "Start 30th June, finish 17th September 2027" rather than "Start 1st June…" for a 28 June start, and "Summer 2027 (May 10th - August 20th, 2027)" rather than "Spring 2027 (January 11th - April 30th, 2027)". "I confirm that the listed dates are suitable for me; 14th June – 22nd August" is answered from it too (No for a 28 June start, and never ticked as an acknowledgement). "Are you graduating before October 2027?" is answered from your graduation date, Capgemini's "Is your undergraduate … degree completed or still predicted?" picks "Predicted" while it runs (and its "If you selected predicted Status, which month…" gets the month, or "N/A" once you've graduated; the same about a postgraduate degree you don't have is left alone), and "School Year completed by the beginning of Summer 2027" picks the year you'll be in just before then ("Junior" in the third year of four). A date box that says "M/d/yyyy" (Microsoft Forms) takes the whole date, and an "Expected Graduation Year" box that opens a calendar ("Pick date...") takes June 2027, not 1 January. A plain box with no format on a page that doesn't say how it writes dates (English with no region, a .com address: Personio's "Available from") gets "28 June 2027", which nobody reads as 6 February.
- **Interview slots.** "Please select ALL dates/times for which you are available" ticks every slot (in a checklist, a multi-select or Teamtailor's menu of ticks) that fits the days, hours and dates you can't do (exams) set under _Interview availability_, weekdays 8am–8pm by default: "Monday 13th October – 10:00-11:00", "Tue 14/10 AM", "w/c 20th October", "Morning (9am-12pm)", UK or US dates, years left out. One choice gets the earliest, "None of these dates work for me" only what's left when nothing fits, and a box asking in words gets "Weekdays, 8am–8pm".
- **US sanctions and export controls.** "Please confirm whether any of the below applies to you… U.S. sanctions and export controls" ticks exactly the statements that are true of you, from your nationality (two work: "British, Irish"), the country and region you live in and whether you'd relocate, or else "None of the above", and its follow-up gets "Not applicable". Yes/No versions ("Are you a citizen or resident of Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, or the Crimea region…?", "…a national of any country subject to US embargo?") too. When your profile can't tell (no nationality, a region of Ukraine, Russia with relocation unknown), it's left for you, and never sent to the AI.
- **Conflicts of interest** that banks and law firms ask: worked there before, relatives or a "Personal Relationship" with an employee ("Are you connected to any current employees of Rothesay?" gets "Not Applicable" after your No), and whether you or a close family member is a government or public official (including "politically exposed person" and state-owned companies). "If yes, please give details" boxes get your details only when you answered Yes, and "…Otherwise, enter N/A" gets "N/A" after a No. "If you were referred by a Graham Capital employee, please enter their name. If not, write N/A." gets "N/A" when where you heard about the job was no referral.
- **Criminal and regulatory history**, once you've answered them in Settings › _Conflicts of interest_: "Have you ever been convicted of a criminal offence?", "…suspended or barred by the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board?", "…any administrative or regulatory charges pending against you; been fined, or had a license revoked?". Left blank, they're left for you. A list of statements to tick ("Do any of the following apply to you?": serving as a government official, post-employment restrictions, a household member in US federal procurement… and "None of these apply to me", Oracle's toggle pills included) is read statement by statement, and "None…" is picked only when your answers make every one a No.
- **Voluntary EEO and UK social-mobility questions.** Gender, detailed ethnicity, veteran, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, neurodivergence; school type, free school meals, a parent's degree (a "highest level of education" list only gets an option your answer settles), household earner's job (which also answers "Which socio-economic background do you identify with?"), postcode at 14, care, caring, refugee background, bursaries. Pick one detailed ethnicity (e.g. _Asian – Chinese_) and each form gets its closest option: "Chinese - Chinese" on a 2011-style UK list, the subgroup on two-step forms, "East Asian" or "Asian" where that's all there is (Bain's "Asian (inclusive of Northeast Asian, Southeast Asian…)" too), and a mixed background ticks both groups on a "select all that apply" list without a mixed option. "Female" picks "Woman", never "Trans Woman" listed before it (Redgate's survey is in alphabetical order). These are opt-in, and "— don't fill —" is the default, except four yes/no questions that are answered No unless your profile says otherwise: "Are you a member of the LGBTQ+ community?" (Yes from your orientation or gender identity), "Are you the first in your family to attend university?" (Yes when no parent has a degree), "…a lower socio-economic household?" (Yes for a working-class household earner's job or free school meals) and "Have you participated in the following organizations?" (the ones your CV names, else "None of the above" or "Prefer not to say"). An answer of _Prefer not to say_ in your profile is kept. Asked in a text box, "How would you describe your ethnic origin?" gets your answer as you gave it, and "Would you consider yourself having any type of impairment? If yes, please describe." your Yes or No (a description is left for you). Northern Ireland's community background (Isio's "Community Background Data (Belfast, Northern Ireland applications)…") gets "Please select if you are not applying for a role based in Northern Ireland" when the job is elsewhere and is yours otherwise; "Did your household receive income support during your school years?" is yours too.

## AI answers

Answers to questions it has never seen, written by the AI provider you choose, with your own key (see [Setting up AI](using.md#setting-up-ai-optional)).

- **Every question the rules leave empty is answered by AI as part of the same fill:** open questions (_"Why do you want to join Figma?"_, _"Describe how Belvedere Trading makes money"_, _"In 150 words, why Real Estate Asset Management?"_), firm-specific choices (_"Which desk interests you most?"_, _"Rank your top areas of finance"_, _"Which engineering work at Figma? First choice"_) and yes/no questions your material settles (_"Do you have practical Python experience?"_, _"Did you take the SAT?"_, _"Are you a member of a university society?"_).
- **Written for this job.** It uses the job's real description, found the same way as for cover letters: the page, the job site's listing, the letter you wrote for this application, or Trackr's company note. It also uses your CV, profile and notes, plus your answer guidance. The earlier answers you gave for other employers are reused as your own words, never with the other firm's name.
- **Extra details for the AI.** Settings › _Skills & writing_: anything an application might ask that isn't in the boxes, in your own words ("no relatives in public office, no convictions, happy to relocate"). The AI reads it when it answers leftover questions and when it writes your cover letter; it never leaves your browser except to your chosen AI provider.
- **Checked like the letters.**
  - A choice must be one of the options.
  - Written answers keep to the limit the question states (words, characters, sentences or the box's own limit).
  - Every number, tool and employer must come from your material or the posting, with no placeholders or stock phrases.
  - A second pass fact-checks written answers against your CV.
  - Anything that fails goes back to the model once; anything that still fails is left for you.
- **What it never does.** Passwords, card and bank details, and marketing or talent-pool opt-ins (a box that says “Keep me updated about future opportunities” included) never leave the page. Unless you switch them on under Settings › _Cover letters & AI_ › _Questions the AI may also answer_, nor do diversity questions, declarations and consent boxes (OC&C's "The information I have provided is accurate", Netcraft's "Please disclose below whether AI tools were used…", numbered or not), ID numbers and your date of birth; criminal, regulatory, vetting and health questions are only answered from what you wrote in _Answer guidance_ (e.g. "I have no convictions"), and your adjustments answer only goes with them. Nor does a follow-up whose condition the answer before it rules out: "If yes, please provide the applicable deadline or timeline." after a No, Maven's "If you selected 'A friend or relative', please put their full name below" after another source, "If your institution is not listed above, please specify below" once your university was found. Your answer to a question it never sees isn’t passed on with the question after it either (a date of birth just before “Why us?”). Facts it can't find (a referrer's name, a past application, a test score) are skipped, not guessed. Grades are never converted: a GPA or class your profile holds is only filled by the rules (a GPA box that wants a number stays empty for a 2:1), and a grade the AI writes must be one your material gives, never the "0-4.0 scale" the question names.
- **Every question, if you want.** Under Settings › _Cover letters & AI_ › _Questions the AI may also answer_ (all off by default, with an _All of them_ switch), each kind can go to the AI with rules of its own:
  - _Diversity and equal opportunity_: answered from your Voluntary disclosures (sent to your provider only then). One you left on “— don’t fill —” gets “Prefer not to say”, “I don’t wish to answer” or “Decline to self-identify”, never a guess from your name, nationality or school. “Did your household receive income support during your school years?” too.
  - _Declarations and consents_: “The information I have provided is accurate”, privacy notices, a typed “Signature” box (your full legal name), and “Please disclose whether AI tools were used…” answered truthfully (AI helped draft your answers). Acknowledgement boxes JobToFill recognises still follow _Tick acknowledgement boxes_. Marketing and talent-pool opt-ins are never ticked or sent.
  - _Government, legal and security vetting_: “Are you, or is any member of your immediate family, a current or former government official?”, “Have you ever been convicted of a criminal offence?”, sanctions, background and credit checks. Answered from your Conflicts of interest answers and answer guidance; where they say nothing, as someone with nothing to declare. A clearance, a citizenship or where you’ve lived is never claimed unless your material settles it.
  - _Health and adjustments_: “Do you require any reasonable adjustments during the recruitment process?” from your profile and guidance, else no adjustments needed.
  - _ID numbers and date of birth_: only a number or date your profile, CV or guidance gives, as written there (“QQ 12 34 56 C” for “QQ123456C”); anything else is left for you.

  When a question of one of these kinds is left for you, the popup says where to switch that kind on.
  - **Diversity and equal opportunity.** Answered from Settings › _Voluntary disclosures_; a question you haven’t answered there gets “Prefer not to say”. Your answers go to your AI provider.
  - **Declarations and consents.** “The information I have provided is accurate”, privacy notices, typed signatures and AI-use disclosures (Netcraft’s “Please disclose below whether AI tools were used…”), answered truthfully. Marketing and talent-pool opt-ins are never ticked.
  - **Government, legal and security vetting.** Public officials and politically exposed persons, criminal and regulatory history, security clearance, background checks and where you’ve lived: answered from your profile and answer guidance and, where they say nothing, as someone with nothing to declare. Only switch it on if that is true of you. It never claims a clearance or a citizenship you don’t list.
  - **Health and adjustments.** Where your profile says nothing: no adjustments needed.
  - **ID numbers and date of birth.** Only numbers in your profile, your CV or your answer guidance, never made up. Passwords and card numbers are never sent.

- **You stay in charge.** AI answers are outlined in dashed orange; the popup lists each one, plus what it left and why. Undo takes them back with the rest of the fill. They're remembered per application, so the next page or a refill doesn't ask again. Switch it off under Settings › _Cover letters & AI_, or use _Answer them with AI_ in the popup for one page.

## Cover letters and CVs

- **Write cover letter** in the popup, on any application. JobToFill finds the job's real description, writes a one-page letter in your voice from your CV, checks every fact, and attaches the PDF to the form (and pastes the text into "cover letter" boxes). The next steps of the same application get it too.
- **Finds the actual job.** It checks the application page itself, the job site's public listing APIs (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, SmartRecruiters, Workable, Oracle…), "View job" links, the job page behind an application address (Meta Careers' `/profile/create_application/{id}/` is `/profile/job_details/{id}/`), the job a sign-in or application step names only in its form (SuccessFactors keeps every step at one session address), the page you came from, the job pages you opened in that tab just before (so sites that send _Apply_ to a bare sign-in address still work) and, if you allow it, your browsing history (a visit whose tab title only names the site, "Meta Careers", still counts by its job ID). Text on the form that isn't about the job (a cookie notice, a diversity self-identification notice, Dayforce's "Job Applicant Privacy Notice") is never taken for its description, and on an application step (an `/apply` page, a sign-in) the job's own page is asked before any text on the step, which counts only as "likely" when nothing else answers and is never used once the finder has turned it down (Greencore's "Question Summary"). On iCIMS the job is read from its frame, not from the employer's pitch around it (Atlassian's "Join the Atlassian Talent Community"), and a bot check's page (Cloudflare's "Sorry, you have been blocked") is never a job. Employers' own careers sites are read too: Google Careers (`/jobs/results/{id}`), TikTok and ByteDance (`/search/{id}`, each section one pre-wrapped text), Brassring's job details, Talentsoft (Crédit Agricole CIB, whose whole page sits in one ASP.NET form), GTI's graduate sites (Word-pasted adverts), Factorial, Avature pages whose only job title is the tab's, Cornerstone OnDemand (`csod.com`) and Kernel ATS (`apptrkr.io`); a sign-in step's `redirect_path=` leads back to the job. Pages that only render with scripts, or that block plain lookups (Meta answers some with HTTP 400), are read in a background tab. Then it **proves it's the same job** (same job ID, company and title) before writing; if it's unsure, the AI double-checks, and you can always paste the job instead.
- **Your style.** Add letters you've written before (PDF, Word or text). New letters copy their voice, structure and length, and reuse what they say about you. They never reuse the company they were written for.
- **Bulletproof facts.** Every draft is checked before you see it, and failures go back to the model to fix:
  - every number must appear in your CV, notes or the posting;
  - tools and skills it claims must be yours;
  - names must be real;
  - it must name the right company, with no other company's name leaking in from an example letter;
  - no placeholders, markdown, clichés or em dashes, and a sensible length.

  A second AI pass reads the letter against your CV and flags any claim about you that isn't backed up. Whatever is still doubtful is listed for you to check.

- **Quick apply.** The small **Quick apply** button next to _Write cover letter_ does all of it without asking. Your details go into the form at once (acknowledgement and privacy-notice boxes ticked too, whatever the setting says; marketing opt-ins never), along with AI answers to the leftover questions. Meanwhile it finds the job, then writes and fits the letter while it tailors your CV alongside (not one after the other), then puts the letter and the tailored CV in, in place of anything already in those fields, and says what it did in a toast on the page (including how many boxes it ticked, so you can untick any you disagree with). There's no need to press _Fill_ while it works: if you do, the CV and letter uploads are left for Quick apply (and a step of an application in steps waits for them). If it stops (no AI credit, say), your usual CV goes in instead. It works in a background tab beside the application and closes it when done; if the job's description can't be found it reads the application page itself. It never submits. A small **Last quick apply** link then appears in the popup: it opens a page with the letter and CV (PDFs, plus the CV as `.tex`) for a look afterwards. Only the latest one is kept, in the browser's memory: it goes when you run another, press _Clear_ or close the browser, and it is never in the backup file. If the AI key, its permission or site access isn't set up yet, the normal studio opens to ask for it.
- **Eligibility check.** If the posting has hard requirements (graduation year, right to work, clearance, degree), it tells you when your profile doesn't seem to meet them.
- **Looks like a LaTeX letter.** The PDF is typeset like the classic LaTeX letter: Latin Modern (Computer Modern) fonts, TeX-style justified paragraphs with hyphenation, your name and a contact line with clickable links, and it always fits on one page. The text stays selectable, so applicant tracking systems can read it.
- **Tailor my CV.** Rewords and reorders your CV around the job's keywords, using only facts already in it. You can start it as soon as the job has been read, while the letter is still being written; the letter is saved with it. It shows keyword coverage before → after and a list of every change. If you choose to, it uploads the tailored PDF instead of your usual CV for that application. Rebuilt from your CV file, a project is just its name and what it is (its technologies are in its bullets and your Skills, not listed under the name), and achievements, skills and languages are rows with a bold label and plain text.
- **Your CV as LaTeX.** Paste your CV's LaTeX in Settings › _Cover letters_. JobToFill reads its structure and tailors only the wording of bullets and the order of what it may touch; names, dates, places, titles and grades are always kept exactly. The tailored CV is drawn like your template as a PDF, and **Download .tex** gives LaTeX you can compile in Overleaf.
- **Edit freely.** The letter is editable, the PDF preview updates as you type, and the checks re-run on your edits. Edits are saved as you type, also when you close the studio straight after (the PDF is laid out from them the next time it opens, or before then when a form gets it or you download it from Settings › _Cover letters & AI_). Use "Rewrite" with an instruction such as "mention my chess" or "shorter".
- **Pick up where you left off.** Coming back to a job you already wrote for offers that letter, with your edits, instead of writing a new one.
- **Bring your own model.** [Claude](https://console.anthropic.com/settings/keys) through Anthropic's own API: Sonnet 5.5, the all-rounder (about 5¢ a letter); Haiku 5.5, the quickest and cheapest (well under 1¢); or Opus 5.5, the most capable (about 10¢). Opus 5.5 has a **fast mode** (Settings › _Cover letters_ › _Fast mode_, a research preview on Anthropic's own API): it writes up to 2.5× as fast for twice the price (about 20¢ a letter); if your key can't use it yet, Claude writes at its usual speed and _Test connection_ says so. Steps that don't need reasoning (reading the job, the letter, the fact check, the CV) run without Claude thinking first, as they already do through OpenRouter, so Sonnet and Haiku start writing straight away. JobToFill keeps its instructions and your material in Claude's prompt cache between the steps of a letter or a page of answers, so the repeated part costs a tenth as much. Or [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai) (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash by default, about a tenth of a cent per letter, or the same Claude models), [DeepSeek's own API](https://platform.deepseek.com/api_keys) (`deepseek-chat`), or any OpenAI-compatible provider. Settings › _Cover letters_ lists each provider's suggested models with what each is good for; "Another model…" takes any id the provider offers. Each provider keeps its own key and model, so you can switch back and forth. With keys for more than one, another takes over when the one you chose is out of credit, rate-limited or down (Claude first; you can switch that off).

## Discover internships (beyond Trackr)

- **Firms nobody advertises.** Toolbar → _Discover internships_ searches a bundled registry of 2,100+ reputable finance firms: SEC-registered advisers (from the SEC's Form ADV data: headcount, assets, private funds) plus the firms in Wikipedia's hedge fund, investment bank, private equity and Tiger Cub categories. Filter by kind (hedge funds, quant, prop trading, asset managers, credit, private equity, boutique advisory, family offices, VC), place and size. Small, well-regarded firms rank first.
- **Leaves out what Trackr already lists.** It reads Trackr's own finance and tech trackers (this season and next) and hides those companies, so what's left are the HBKs and Lansdownes.
- **Firms like the ones you name.** "HBK, Lansdowne Partners" finds firms in the same niche, city and size, and firms that share their Wikipedia categories.
- **Checked live, not guessed.** _Check careers_ reads the firm's own website: its careers pages, the job board they embed or link to (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable, Workday, SmartRecruiters, Recruitee, Phenom, Oleeo), and lists only student roles (internships, summer analyst, off-cycle, insight and spring weeks, graduate). Firms without a job board get the programme sentences from their careers page with the deadline and the application email (Cloudflare-hidden addresses decoded). A board is only guessed when the site doesn't name one, and a guessed board counts only if it carries the firm's name.
- **AI ideas, verified.** With an AI key, _Ask the AI for more ideas_ adds firms the registry doesn't know, but only those whose website loads and names them **and** which the SEC adviser register or Wikipedia confirms. The rest are listed as left out, with the reason.
- **Watchlist.** _Watch_ a firm and JobToFill re-checks its board and careers pages every six hours. New roles and programme pages show as a number on the toolbar button and in the popup; a role listed all along never comes back as new, and _Mark as seen_ or _Remove_ holds even while a check is running. Small firms post once a year, often quietly.
- `npm run firms` rebuilds the registry from the latest SEC file and Wikipedia (`FIRMS_CONTACT=you@example.org`, which the SEC requires).

## General autofill

- Checkout and shipping forms, including country/state codes, phone numbers and split names. A "Company (optional)" box on a shipping form is _not_ filled with your employer.
- **Passwords and cards** (Settings › _Passwords & cards_), filled straight away on every fill, Quick apply and sign-in step.
  - Sign-up pages: a unique strong password is generated for "Create password" + "Confirm password" ("Choose Password" / "Retype Password", "Verify New Password"…) and saved for that site, so a Workday account for every employer is no longer painful. Choosing a single default password instead is also an option.
  - **Passwords that fit the page.** The rules a sign-up page states ("at least 8 characters", "not longer than 18", "one upper case and one lower case letter", "a number or punctuation character", "no spaces", the special characters it lists or bans, and the box's own `minlength` / `maxlength` / `pattern`) are read, and a generated password always meets them. When your default password wouldn't, it isn't submitted to be refused: a fitting one is made, saved for that site, and the fill says so.
  - Login pages: the saved password for that site is filled, or your default password when you chose it and nothing is saved for the site. Employers that share one host (every SuccessFactors company on `career8.successfactors.com`) each get their own login, and a page there that doesn't say which employer gets the login saved for the host itself before one saved for an employer.
  - Checkout pages: the selected card is filled, including inside payment-provider iframes such as Stripe.
  - Only where you'd see them, and only for the page's own site: a password or card number never goes into a box smaller than 8 × 8 pixels or faded below 10%, and a frame from another site (a chat widget's, an ad's) gets neither your default password nor a new one, only a login saved for that very site. A job portal's own frame (an iCIMS application framed on the employer's careers site) is the page's own; cards still only go to payment providers' frames.
- **Right-click menu.** _Fill this page_, _Insert from profile → Email / LinkedIn / …_ into any box, _Generate strong password_ and _Insert verification code from email_ (into the box you right-clicked: one that hasn't taken the code is said so, and the code stays unused for the next try).

## Job-portal accounts

- **Signs in and creates portal accounts for you** (Settings › _Sign in and create job-portal accounts for me_, on by default). When you fill (button, shortcut, right-click or Quick apply) a secure page that is only a sign-in or sign-up form, with no job questions or uploads on it:
  - a sign-in page for a site with a saved login gets it, and its _Sign in_ button is clicked;
  - a sign-in page for a site with no known account gets its _Create account_ / _Register_ / _New user?_ link clicked, and the sign-up page that opens in the tab is filled by itself;
  - a page that only offers ways in (Workday's _Sign in with Google / Apple / LinkedIn_ … _Sign in with email_) gets _Sign in with email_, never another service;
  - a sign-up page is filled (your default or a fitting new password, the sign-up form's own terms box ticked, never marketing) and its _Create account_ button clicked;
  - the code the site then emails is typed in from your inbox (with Nylas connected), and that step's _Continue_ / _Verify_ clicked; a site that emails a link instead ("An email has been sent to you. Please verify your account.") gets that link opened in a tab beside it, which closes again, and then you're signed in;
  - "An account with this email already exists" goes back to sign in with the saved or default password. Workday never says so: a known email is just sent back to its sign-in page (a new one gets "An email has been sent to you"), so that is read as the account existing, and the new password made for the sign-up page is dropped (it can't be the account's) in favour of your default one;
  - a sign-in a saved or made-up password doesn't get through is tried once more with your default password, which is then kept for the site; when that is refused too, the toast says the account exists and points to _Forgot your password?_ (never clicked for you), and no login made on the way is left saved;
  - the application page it lands on is filled as usual (once the page has finished drawing), and the toast lists every button it clicked.
- **CAPTCHAs are yours.** A plain "I'm not a robot" checkbox is ticked like any other box. A real CAPTCHA (reCAPTCHA, hCaptcha, Turnstile, Arkose, an image CAPTCHA) is never clicked, solved or bypassed: the toast asks you to complete it (and to accept a "Terms of Use" statement that opens in a dialog), and JobToFill carries on as soon as you have, for up to five minutes.
- **Strict about what it clicks.** Only buttons and links whose wording says sign in, log in, create account, register, sign up, "New user?", "Already have an account? Sign in", "Sign in with email", or _Continue_ / _Verify_ on an emailed-code step; a bare _Submit_ / _Next_ only when it is the one submit of a pure sign-in or sign-up form (or laid over that form's own _Sign in_ button, as Workday does). Never anything that says apply, application, submit application, send, withdraw, delete, pay, review and submit, forgot or reset password, upload, cancel, or signs in with another service (Google, LinkedIn, Microsoft, Indeed…). Never on a page with job-application questions, never while a required box is empty or an error is showing, never on `http://` pages, and only what is on top at the button's spot (nothing hidden under a banner). Each tab's run lasts ten minutes at most, takes at most six clicks, stays on the same site (and employer), and stops when you go elsewhere.
- **Dropdowns on account pages.** SuccessFactors' "Country/Region Code" gets "UNITED KINGDOM (+44)" (not the Isle of Man or Jersey, and "+1" is the United States unless you live in Canada), the phone number then goes in without its code, and "Country/Region of Residence" gets your country, not your state.

## Verification codes from email

- **Codes fill themselves.** Job portals want an account, then "the 6-digit code we just emailed you", again and again. Connect your inbox once through [Nylas](https://www.nylas.com) (Settings › _Email codes_: paste one API key) and JobToFill watches for code boxes, waits for that email, and types the code in: one box or a row of single-digit boxes, React widgets and Oracle's six round pin boxes (which have no length limit and keep only the first digit typed into each) included. "Check your inbox to verify your email" pages get the link from that email opened in a new tab.
- **Reads emails like a person.** It finds the code beside "verification code", "one-time passcode", "Bestätigungscode" and friends, and ignores order numbers, phone numbers, prices, years, dates, tracking links and the hidden preview text. The box decides the shape: six boxes take a six-digit code, a numeric box no letters.
- **Only from the site that asked.** A code is filled by itself only when the email comes from that site: the same domain, the same portal (a Workday page and a Workday email; Oracle, SuccessFactors, iCIMS, Greenhouse and two dozen more), the company's own domain behind its portal (BNY's people.bny.com for its Oracle site eofe.fa.us2.oraclecloud.com/…/sites/BNY-Careers, whose address only names the employer in its path), or an email that links back to the page. On a portal many employers share, a page that names its employer (acme.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com, jobs.smartrecruiters.com/Acme) only takes the portal's email when that names the same employer too (Workday's from acme@myworkday.com, “Your Acme verification code”), so another employer's code, sent while you apply there in another tab, never goes into this one. A code from anyone else is only offered with a _Use it_ button, so a look-alike page can't collect your bank's sign-in code. Secure pages only; each code is used once. The code watcher itself submits nothing: only _Sign in and create job-portal accounts for me_, during a sign-in or sign-up it is running, presses the code step's _Continue_.
- Right-click any box › _Insert verification code from email_ takes the newest code whoever sent it.

## Also

- Works inside cross-origin iframes (embedded Greenhouse boards) and open shadow DOM (web components).
- **Show detected fields** labels every field with what JobToFill thinks it is: green means ready, amber means your profile lacks it, grey means unknown.
- **Undo** puts back whatever was on the page before the fills since the last Undo, each box as it was before the first of them: pressing Fill twice, or Quick apply’s two fills, are taken back together with the AI’s answers. Pressing **Alt+Shift+F** again while a fill runs doesn’t start a second one beside it (a fill of another kind, like Quick apply’s letter and CV, waits its turn), so Undo still has everything.
- **Page map.** Toolbar → _Page map_ opens, in a tab beside the page, the page as JobToFill and the AI see it: every field numbered (f1, f2…) with its question, options, what’s picked and what JobToFill takes it for (“→ name.first ✓”, “✗ no value” when your profile has no answer, “(none: for AI)” when no rule knows the question), the buttons and frames, and the form’s step, alerts and dialogs. Click a line and the page scrolls to that field and outlines it; _Only fields that need something_ cuts a long form down to what’s missing, invalid or left for the AI. _Copy for an AI chat_ and _Save as text_ give the same map with your details replaced by placeholders, as in a bug-report snapshot.
- **Save a snapshot for a bug report.** When a form trips JobToFill up, the link at the bottom of the popup saves one HTML file: a copy of the form with your values and personal details taken out (see [Privacy](privacy.md)), and a trace of what JobToFill saw and did with each field (what it took each question for, how it wrote the answer, what the page said). Attach it to a bug report and the form can be rebuilt as a test, without anyone needing your login.
- Only empty fields are filled unless you turn on _Overwrite_, and filled fields are outlined. One exception: a country the site chose for you is put right. Workday picks the _Country / Territory_ and the phone code's country from where your connection seems to be (Italy on a VPS there); when that isn't your country it becomes yours, the chip "Italy (+39)" becomes "United Kingdom (+44)", and the address fields are filled once the page has switched to that country's (_County_ and _City_ instead of _Province_ and _City (Comune)_).
- Light and dark mode, an automatic backup file, and import/export.
